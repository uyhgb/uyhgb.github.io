可以。liquid transfer 更简单，也更容易临场说出来。下面给你一版全文，我顺便把几处句子再简化了一点，尽量保持简单、稳、容易记。

Good afternoon, professors. My name is Yiqing Wang. I am an undergraduate student majoring in Biomedical Engineering at Beihang University.

I have cross-field experience in wet-lab research, robotics, and machine learning. During my undergraduate study, I gradually found that I am especially interested in how AI and robots can be used in real scientific experiments.

First, I would like to introduce my robotics experience.

In a RoboCup project, I mainly worked on the integration and deployment of a robot vision system. We used a Jetson Orin NX as the edge computing platform, and deployed a YOLOv5-based vision module with ROS and Docker. My main work was to solve engineering problems such as software compatibility, camera access, and communication between different containers. This project gave me my first experience in connecting AI models with a real robot system.

Later, I worked on a hip exoskeleton project. In this project, I independently built the main software pipeline from sensing to motor control. IMU data were collected by an ESP32 and sent through ROS2. An SVR model was used to estimate the gait phase online, and the result was then converted into torque commands for a Unitree motor. I also added torque limits and a safe reset function. This project helped me understand how sensing, model inference, communication, and physical execution need to work together in a real system.

At the same time, I also have a strong wet-lab background.

In a national undergraduate research project, I studied how X-ray pretreatment and simulated microgravity affected bacterial spore revival. I participated in wet-lab experiments, microscopic counting, data analysis, and paper writing. We later submitted a paper to Acta Astronautica, and I am a co-first author.

I also worked on a project that combined machine learning with wet-lab experiments. Because we had only a small number of experimental samples, we used Bayesian Ridge regression and a constrained UCB strategy to recommend promising experimental conditions. More importantly, we tested the recommended conditions again in real experiments. So this project formed a simple closed loop from experiment to model and back to experiment.

These experiences made me interested in scientific embodied intelligence and laboratory automation.

I think my main advantage is my cross-field background. I may not have the same depth in pure programming as computer science students, or the same depth in robotics as automation students. However, I have real experience in both wet-lab experiments and robotic systems.

For example, in a liquid transfer task, moving one hundred microliters of liquid from one container to another is not enough. We also need to know whether the liquid was really taken up, whether there are bubbles or sediment, whether the sample needs to be mixed, and whether contamination has happened.

So I hope to study how experimental knowledge, process rules, system states, and abnormal conditions can be translated into tasks that AI agents and robots can understand and execute.

In the future, I hope to work on scientific embodied intelligence, laboratory agents, and intelligent scientific instruments, and combine my experience in experiments, AI, and robotics.

Thank you for your attention.

background → robotics → wet lab → machine learning → why this field → why me → future
